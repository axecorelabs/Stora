Cover note for counsel

Draft 1 · 12 October 2026

Three drafts follow: Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, and a short Disclaimer, written specifically for how Lagos Life Forbes actually works — not a generic template with the product name swapped in. Lagos Life Forbes is treated throughout as the platform brand, not as a leaderboard for one specific game: it runs time-limited Campaigns, each centered on a featured third-party game or app named when that Campaign is announced. Lagos Life was the first; others are expected to follow, and none of the text below needs to change when a new one launches. Six things are flagged for your attention before anything goes live.

Flagged for review

  1. 1. "Lagos Life Forbes" as a multi-game platform brand

    The business plan is now to run Campaigns across multiple third-party games, not just Lagos Life — but the brand name and every live domain (lagoslifeforbes.com.ng, lagoslifegames.com.ng) still say "Lagos Life." The drafts below treat Lagos Life Forbes as the platform's name, with each game (Lagos Life included) as one featured game among possibly several, rather than rewriting the brand around whichever game is current. That's a workable legal fiction — plenty of platforms keep a name that's no longer literally descriptive — but it's a deliberate choice, not a default, and worth your sign-off before scaling to a second game. If a parent brand separate from "Lagos Life Forbes" is preferred instead, these drafts would need the entity/brand split redone.

  2. 2. Real-money rewards tied to a self-reported stat

    Campaigns can advertise a cash reward (the live site has run one for ₦100,000) for whoever reports the best claimed result for that Campaign's game (e.g. highest in-game balance). Because the result is self-reported and entirely unverifiable against the actual game, there's a real question of whether this reads as a skill-based competition (fine) or edges toward a game of chance / informal lottery under Nigerian law (Lottery Act, National Lottery Regulatory Commission, and any applicable Lagos State gaming rules). This question applies identically to every future game, not just Lagos Life. Worth a direct read on whether the Terms need explicit "skill-based contest, no purchase necessary, judged by submission plausibility and admin review" language to stay clearly on the right side of that line, and whether any state-level promotion/lottery permit is actually required before advertising a cash prize publicly.

  3. 3. Trademark exposure, and it multiplies per game

    The product name, domains, and copy all use "Lagos Life" directly (Lagos Life Forbes, lagoslifeforbes.com.ng, lagoslifegames.com.ng), and every future Campaign will name another third party's game the same way. The Disclaimer draft states each one is unofficial and unaffiliated, but a disclaimer reduces confusion risk — it doesn't grant permission to use someone else's mark. Worth a repeatable internal check (e.g. a short trademark-risk look before naming any new game in a Campaign) rather than handling this one game at a time, and whether reaching out to Lagos Life's developers directly (even informally) is worth doing before this gets more public.

  4. 4. Minimum age, given real payments are involved

    The product itself has no age gate today. Ads, sponsor slots, and social-handle unlocks all run real Paystack transactions. Drafted a floor of 18 for anyone making a payment and 13+ for general use below — want your view on whether that's the right split, and whether we need an actual age-gate in the product (not just text in the Terms) before this is defensible.

  5. 5. Operating entity

    Drafted as operated by Axecore Labs Limited (RC 9466911), the same entity behind Stora (stora.com.ng), since Lagos Life Forbes is built and hosted as a Stora side-project with "Hosted by Stora" branding on every page. Flag if this should actually sit under a different entity or a separate registration — worth revisiting again once a second game's Campaign is live and this is clearly a multi-game product, not a one-off side project.

  6. 6. NDPA depth

    The Privacy Policy draft follows the same Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 framing the main Stora privacy policy already uses, and is accurate to what the product actually collects and stores (confirmed against the live code, not guessed). The data model doesn't change per game (same fields regardless of which game a Campaign is about), so this holds up across future games without rework. It's still a directional first pass, not a full NDPA compliance audit — whether this needs registration with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission as a data controller, given the volume expected, is worth a direct answer from you.

Anything in brackets throughout the three drafts is a placeholder — a fact I didn't have confirmed and didn't want to invent. Everything else (entity name, RC number, governing law, support email, payment processor, what data is actually collected) is pulled directly from the live product and the rest of the Stora codebase, not assumed.

One product note, not a legal one: the live site's footer disclaimer today still says "not affiliated with Lagos Life's developers" specifically, because only one game exists so far. Once a second game's Campaign launches, that on-page line should be updated to name the right game for each Campaign (or generalized the way the Disclaimer draft below is) — otherwise the live site and these documents will say different things.

Privacy Policy

Draft — last updated 12 October 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Axecore Labs Limited (RC 9466911), operating Lagos Life Forbes as part of the Stora platform, collects, uses, and shares information when you use the Service. We are the data controller for the personal data described below, in accordance with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA).

The short version that matters most: your display name, your in-game username/handle, avatar, your claimed Campaign entry, and (if you've unlocked them) your social media handles are shown publicly to anyone who visits the Service. Your email, password, and IP address are never shown publicly.

1. Information we collect

If you create an account:

  • Email address and password (stored as a salted hash — we never store or can see your actual password).
  • Display name and your in-game username/handle for whichever game's Campaign you enter.
  • An avatar image, if you choose to upload one.
  • Any social media handles you add (Instagram, TikTok, X, Snapchat) — these are only shown publicly if you've purchased an active social-handle unlock.

Whether or not you have an account:

  • A screenshot supporting your claimed entry, submitted with every submission, and the claimed value, name, and handle you enter alongside it.
  • Your IP address, which we hash with a secret salt before storing it — we use the hash only to enforce submission limits and detect abuse, and we cannot reverse it back into your real IP address.
  • A random visitor ID stored in a first-party cookie, used only to avoid double-counting your own visits in our visitor statistics — not used for cross-site tracking or advertising.

If you make a payment (an ad, a sponsor placement, or a social-handle unlock): your payment is processed entirely by Paystack. We receive confirmation that payment succeeded and the amount, but we never receive or store your card number or banking details.

2. How we use your information

  • To operate the leaderboard: reviewing and displaying approved submissions, computing rank, and running Campaigns.
  • To prevent abuse: rate-limiting submissions, detecting bot traffic (via Cloudflare Turnstile), and investigating fraudulent or manipulated entries.
  • To process payments for ads, sponsor placements, and social-handle unlocks.
  • To communicate with you about your submission, account, or a Campaign you've entered.
  • To maintain and improve the Service.

3. Legal basis for processing

We process your information based on: your consent (for example, submitting a screenshot, or linking a social handle), performance of a contract (operating your account, processing a payment), and our legitimate interest in preventing fraud and abuse on the Service.

4. Who we share information with

  • Paystack, to process payments.
  • Cloudflare, which hosts the Service, stores submitted screenshots and avatars, and provides bot protection (Turnstile) and our visitor-count infrastructure. Cloudflare processes this data on our behalf as a service provider.
  • We do not sell your personal data, and we do not share it with third parties for their own marketing purposes.

5. What's public

The following is visible to any visitor of the Service, with no login required: your display name, your in-game username/handle, avatar (if you've set one), your claimed entry, your rank, and — only while active — your linked social media handles. Approved screenshots themselves are not displayed publicly; they're only visible to our review staff.

6. Cookies

We use a small number of first-party cookies: a session cookie if you're logged in, and a random visitor ID used only for our own visitor-count statistics. We don't use third-party advertising or tracking cookies.

7. Data retention

We keep your account and submission history for as long as your account is active. Rejected submissions and their screenshots may be deleted from our storage. If you ask us to delete your account, we will remove your personal data within a reasonable time, except where we're required to keep records (for example, payment records) for legal or accounting reasons.

The Service is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has created an account or submitted information, contact us and we'll remove it.

10. Security

Passwords are hashed (never stored in plain text); IP addresses are salted and hashed before storage; all traffic to the Service is encrypted (HTTPS); uploaded screenshots and avatars are stored in a private file store, not publicly browsable. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure, and we can't guarantee absolute security.
